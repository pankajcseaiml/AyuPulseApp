AyuPulse — Performance Metrics & Model Evaluation

A multi-metric evaluation framework designed for clinical reliability. Traditional accuracy is misleading for medical datasets with class imbalance; we prioritize AUC-ROC, Recall, and the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC).

📐 Evaluation Metrics Explained

Each metric captures a different dimension of model performance critical to clinical decision-making.

📈

AUC-ROC

Area Under ROC Curve

Threshold-independent discrimination. An AUC of 0.92 means AyuPulse correctly ranks a random diseased patient higher than a random healthy patient 92% of the time.

🎯

Recall (Sensitivity)

TP / (TP + FN)

Proportion of actual positives correctly identified. Missing a true positive can be fatal in heart disease screening — high recall ensures we catch at-risk patients.

⚖️

F1-Score

2 × (P × R) / (P + R)

Harmonic mean of precision and recall. Balances catching diseased patients against avoiding unnecessary alarm — critical when both error types carry consequences.

🧬

MCC

(TP×TN − FP×FN) / √[…]

The only metric that scores high only if the model performs well across all four confusion-matrix quadrants. Robust to class imbalance — the gold standard for medical AI.

🔍 Key Observations

Analysis of the multimodal fusion approach and its clinical implications.

1

Synergistic Multimodal Effect

The AyuPulse multimodal fusion model achieves a +11% improvement in AUC-ROC over the best unimodal model (CXR-only at 0.83 → Multimodal at 0.92). Each modality captures complementary pathophysiological signals: clinical biomarkers (cholesterol, BP, glucose) capture systemic risk; chest X-rays reveal structural cardiac changes (cardiomegaly, pulmonary edema); ECG signals detect electrical conduction abnormalities (arrhythmias, ST-segment changes) invisible to labs and static imaging.

2

Clinical Recall is Paramount

AyuPulse achieves 91% sensitivity, meaning fewer than 1 in 10 patients with heart disease would be missed. In a screening context, the cost of a false negative far outweighs the cost of a false positive — a missed diagnosis can delay life-saving intervention.

3

MCC Confirms Robustness

The MCC of 0.84 (range: −1 to +1, where 0 = random guessing) confirms strong performance across all four confusion-matrix quadrants. Unlike accuracy, MCC is not inflated by the negative-class majority typical in population screening datasets.

4

Confidence Calibration

The fusion model's confidence scoring aligns higher confidence with higher accuracy. Three-modality predictions achieve confidence ≥ 0.80 + (1 − σ) × 0.15, where σ is the standard deviation of individual modality scores. When all three models agree, confidence exceeds 0.90.

📋 Evaluation Protocol

Rigorous methodology ensuring reproducible and statistically sound results.

✂️

Dataset Split

70% training / 30% held-out test, stratified by disease label to preserve prevalence ratios.

🔁

Cross-Validation

5-fold stratified cross-validation on the training set for hyperparameter tuning; final metrics reported on the untouched test fold.

📊

Statistical Significance

95% confidence intervals computed via bootstrap resampling (1,000 iterations) on test-set predictions.

🎚️

Threshold Selection

Operating threshold (default: 0.5) selected to maximize F1-score on the validation set. Adjustable for screening vs. diagnostic use.

⚠️ Limitations & Future Work

Transparent acknowledgment of current boundaries and planned improvements.

🌍

Dataset Diversity

Current evaluation uses a single-source dataset. Multi-center validation across diverse populations (ethnicities, age groups, comorbidities) is planned for generalizability assessment.

🏥

Prospective Validation

Metrics reported here are from retrospective data. A prospective clinical study is required before real-world deployment to confirm efficacy in live clinical workflows.

📉

Calibration Error

Expected calibration error (ECE) and reliability diagrams will be incorporated in future evaluation cycles to ensure risk scores represent well-calibrated probabilities.

🧠

Explainability–Performance Trade-off

The current rule-based clinical model provides high interpretability but may sacrifice some predictive power versus a learned model. Future iterations may explore knowledge-distilled neural clinical models.
